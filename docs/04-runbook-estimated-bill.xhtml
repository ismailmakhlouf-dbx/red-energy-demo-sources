When a customer says their bill is wrong

  1. Check whether it is actually an estimate. Do not take the customer's word or your own assumption — look it up.
  2. Explain it plainly. The meter reading was not available, this bill is an estimate, it will be corrected on the next bill, and they will not be charged twice.
  3. Stop any collections activity on that bill. 61 bills are currently both estimated and being chased. Chasing those is a conduct risk.
  4. Do not open with a goodwill credit. It implies fault, and at 71 affected households it is expensive at scale. Lead with the explanation and the correction guarantee. Credit only if the customer is genuinely out of pocket.
  5. Escalate the high-value, at-risk ones. 22 affected customers were already at high risk of leaving. Those get a specialist, not the script.

What not to say

Do not describe an estimated bill as final, do not blame the customer's meter, and do not quote an internal risk or value score to a customer.
